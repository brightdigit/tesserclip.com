<HelpHeading id="getting-started"><PlayIcon slot="icon" />Getting started</HelpHeading>

1. Copy some text in any app.
2. Open TesserClip: launch it, click its Dock icon, or switch to it with <Kbd keys="⌘Tab" />. A window shows the text you copied, with the cursor at the end.
3. Make your changes.
4. Click **Copy All** in the toolbar, or press <Kbd keys="⇧⌘C" />. You can also select some text and press <Kbd keys="⌘C" />.
5. Switch back to the other app with <Kbd keys="⌘Tab" />, or hide TesserClip with <Kbd keys="⌘H" />, and paste with <Kbd keys="⌘V" />.

<NoteCallout><ClipboardIcon slot="icon" />The first time you open TesserClip, macOS may need your permission before TesserClip can read the clipboard. <a href="/help/clipboard-permission/" class="link-inline">Allow TesserClip to read the clipboard<ChevronIcon /></a></NoteCallout>

<HelpHeading id="windows"><WindowsIcon slot="icon" />Windows and drafts</HelpHeading>

Each TesserClip window is its own draft. It holds the text that was on the clipboard when it opened, and nothing replaces it after that.

<div class="table-scroll">

| When you…                                                  | TesserClip…                                            |
| ---------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| Open TesserClip with no windows open                       | Opens a new window with the text on the clipboard.     |
| Switch to TesserClip with windows open                     | Brings your windows forward. Your text isn’t replaced. |
| Choose <MenuPath items={['File', 'New Window']} /> <Kbd keys="⌘N" /> | Opens a new window with the text on the clipboard.     |

</div>

### Window titles

A window’s title is the first few words of its text. Under the title you’ll see where and when the text was copied, like “Safari · Oct 27 at 1:49 PM”. When the window has changes, macOS shows “Edited” next to the title.

### When there’s new text to open

Copy something else while TesserClip is open and a dot appears on the **New Window from Clipboard** button in every window. Click it, or press <Kbd keys="⌘N" />, to open the new text. TesserClip checks that the clipboard has text without reading it.

### Closing and quitting

If you close a window with edits you haven’t copied, TesserClip asks “Discard your edits?” first. Choose **Cancel** (<Kbd keys="esc" />) to keep the window, or **Discard** (<Kbd keys="return" />) to close it. If you’ve copied the text since your last change, the window closes without asking.

Quitting never asks. Your windows, with their text, come back the next time you open TesserClip. Closing the last window leaves TesserClip running; click its Dock icon to open a new window.

<HelpHeading id="editor"><TextIcon slot="icon" />The editor</HelpHeading>

- **Plain text only.** Anything you paste in becomes plain text.
- **Substitutions are off.** Smart quotes, smart dashes, smart links, data detectors and text replacement are off. Turn any of them on in <MenuPath items={['Edit', 'Substitutions']} />. TesserClip remembers your choices.
- **Spelling is checked** as you type. Misspelled words are underlined; nothing is changed for you.
- **The usual Mac text tools work:** Undo and Redo, Find (<Kbd keys="⌘F" />), Transformations, Speech, Services, and dragging text in and out.

If the clipboard doesn’t contain text, such as an image or a file, the window opens empty. Type, paste with <Kbd keys="⌘V" />, or copy some text and choose <MenuPath items={['File', 'New Window']} />.

<HelpHeading id="permission"><ClipboardIcon slot="icon" />Clipboard permission</HelpHeading>

macOS asks before an app reads the clipboard on its own. Allow TesserClip once in Privacy & Security and it can show your copied text right away. Without it, you can still paste into a window with <Kbd keys="⌘V" />.

<HelpHeading id="faq"><QuestionIcon slot="icon" />Questions</HelpHeading>

<FaqItem question="Why is my new window empty?" open>
  <p>Either the clipboard doesn’t contain text, or TesserClip doesn’t have permission to read it yet. You can paste with <Kbd keys="⌘V" /> any time. To let TesserClip read the clipboard, see <a href="/help/clipboard-permission/" class="link-inline">Clipboard permission</a>.</p>
</FaqItem>
<FaqItem question="I copied something new. Why didn’t my window change?">
  <p>That’s on purpose: a window never replaces the text you’re working on. Click <strong class="font-semibold text-brand-text">New Window from Clipboard</strong> or press <Kbd keys="⌘N" /> to open the new text in its own window.</p>
</FaqItem>
<FaqItem question="Does TesserClip keep a clipboard history?">
  <p>No. TesserClip reads the clipboard only when it opens a window, and only keeps the text of the windows you have open.</p>
</FaqItem>
<FaqItem question="Can TesserClip paste into the other app for me?">
  <p>No. Copy your text with <Kbd keys="⇧⌘C" />, switch back, and paste with <Kbd keys="⌘V" />.</p>
</FaqItem>
<FaqItem question="How do I save text to a file?">
  <p>TesserClip doesn’t save files. Copy the text and paste it into the app where you want to keep it.</p>
</FaqItem>
<FaqItem question="Why aren’t my quotes turning curly?">
  <p>Substitutions are off so your text isn’t changed behind your back. Turn on Smart Quotes in <MenuPath items={['Edit', 'Substitutions']} />. TesserClip remembers the setting.</p>
</FaqItem>
