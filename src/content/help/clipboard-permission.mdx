## Why macOS asks

Your clipboard can hold anything you’ve copied, including passwords and private messages. So macOS lets you decide which apps can read it without you pasting.

Pasting is always up to you. When you press <Kbd keys="⌘V" /> in a TesserClip window, macOS doesn’t ask, and the text appears as usual.

## Allow TesserClip

<HowToSteps>
  <HowToStep title="Open Privacy & Security">
    <p>In the TesserClip window that asks for permission, click <strong class="font-semibold text-brand-text">Open Privacy & Security</strong>. If you closed that window, choose <MenuPath items={['Help', 'Clipboard Permission…']} class="whitespace-normal!" /> to open it again.</p>
  </HowToStep>
  <HowToStep title="Find TesserClip">
    <p>System Settings opens to the list of apps that can paste from other apps.</p>
    <PathChip items={PANE_PATH} />
  </HowToStep>
  <HowToStep title="Allow it">
    <p>Next to TesserClip, choose <strong class="font-semibold text-brand-text">{ALLOW_LABEL}</strong>.</p>
  </HowToStep>
  <HowToStep title="Open your text">
    <p>Switch back to TesserClip and choose <MenuPath items={['File', 'New Window']} /> (<Kbd keys="⌘N" />). The new window shows the text you copied.</p>
  </HowToStep>
</HowToSteps>

<NoteCallout><QuestionIcon slot="icon" /><strong class="font-semibold text-brand-text">Chose Not Now?</strong> That’s fine. Windows open empty, and you can paste into them with <Kbd keys="⌘V" />. Change your mind any time with <MenuPath items={['Help', 'Clipboard Permission…']} /></NoteCallout>

## What TesserClip reads

- TesserClip reads the clipboard only when it opens a window.
- To show the dot on **New Window from Clipboard**, it checks that the clipboard has text, without reading the text.
- Your text stays on your Mac. See the [Privacy Policy](/privacy/).

## Turn it off

Go back to <MenuPath items={PANE_PATH} class="whitespace-normal!" /> and change TesserClip’s setting. TesserClip keeps working; new windows just open empty.
